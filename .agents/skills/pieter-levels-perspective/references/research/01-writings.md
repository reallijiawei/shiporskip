# Pieter Levels (@levelsio) -- Writings & Published Works Research

## Overview

Pieter Levels (born 1986) is a Dutch self-taught developer, entrepreneur, and digital nomad.
He has built and launched over 40 startups, most as solo bootstrapped projects.
His major products include Nomad List, Remote OK, PhotoAI, InteriorAI, and Hoodmaps.
Combined revenue exceeds $3M/year with up to 95% profit margins, built mostly solo.

Primary channels: blog (levels.io), Twitter/X (@levelsio), and his book *MAKE*.

## 1. MAKE: The Indie Maker Handbook (2018)

### Overview

Published March 2018, launched on Product Hunt.
Subtitled "The Indie Maker Handbook."
Website: readmake.com
A practical guide for solo founders to build profitable online businesses without investors.

### Core Formula

**Idea x Execution = Business**

A mediocre idea with great execution ($1,000,000) beats a great idea with no execution ($0).

### Chapter-by-Chapter Key Concepts

#### On Ideas & Problem-Solving

- "Get an idea from problems in your own life. If you don't have problems that are original enough, become a more original person."
- "You are the greatest expert at your own problems" -- you know your problems better than anyone else.
- "Always start from the problem, not the solution" -- companies that start from solutions build things nobody needs.
- "People won't steal your idea if they hear it. And even if they do, they probably won't execute it as well as you."
- He advocates making a list of problems in your daily life and ideas to solve them.

#### On Building & Tech Stack

- "Build your idea with the tools you already know. Don't spend a year learning some language you'll never use."
- On PHP: "Facebook is built on PHP" and "Twitter was built on Ruby" -- the language doesn't matter, shipping does.
- "The people discussing what programming language is best are not shipping products."
- He uses PHP, jQuery, SQLite, and minimal infrastructure -- keeping things simple and proven.
- On learning to code: "Try to build your idea with HTML and CSS and JavaScript and see how far you get. Just Google every single thing you don't know."
- He advocates SQLite as a database, noting it's "faster than the filesystem" and sufficient for most startups.
- "If you have an idea and can't wait to code it up, that's a good sign. If you can wait, it's not a good sign."

#### On MVPs & Shipping

- "Don't spend a year building a big product nobody wants. Launch early and iterate."
- On MVPs: they don't need to be ugly -- they should look professional but only include core functionality.
- He built the first version of Nomads.com in one day and launched it.

#### On Outsourcing vs. DIY

- "Don't outsource building to other people, that's a competitive disadvantage."
- He strongly prefers building it yourself: outsourcing is expensive, creates dependency, and you lose control.
- On VC-funded companies: he laid out a lengthy comparison showing bootstrapping beats VC in terms of founder freedom and odds of profitability.

#### On Growth & Launching

- "Launch early and multiple times" -- to Product Hunt, Hacker News, Reddit, and niche communities.
- Launch in a friendly way: "here's something I made that might be useful for you" rather than pretending to be a big startup.
- "Grow organically. A great product that people really need... will pull people in. You don't need ads."
- "Don't hire people if there's no revenue yet. Don't hire many people if there's revenue either."

#### On Monetization

- "Monetize by asking users for money. Don't sell their data. Don't put ads everywhere."
- "Be honest that you need money to build the product they love and they'll be fine paying for it."
- His revenue math: 1,000 customers x $83/month = $1M/year.

#### On Niches

- "Niches are specific market segments that are shallow enough to easily access, with not too many startups in them."
- Start micro-niche (e.g., booking for hairdressers on Amazon), then expand to adjacent markets.
- He cites how Facebook started as a Harvard-only app and Amazon began as an online bookstore.

#### On Automation & AI

- "Automate by writing programs that do stuff that you do repeatedly. Only automate if it's worth the time saved."
- With AI: "It's now possible to automate almost any process in your company" -- enabling the first billion-dollar single-founder company.

#### On Bootstrapping Philosophy

- "I want to make bootstrapping great (again) #MBGA"
- "Bootstrapping doesn't exclude going big later. VC investors LOVE companies that already have proven revenue."
- "Just... relax" -- the goal is enough money to live from, with freedom and less stress.

#### On Ethics

- "Be ethical, and don't cut corners on ethics" -- no spamming, manipulating users, or faking social media.
- "Just being ethical and nice is a competitive advantage these days because most companies (and people) are not!"

#### On Persistence & Failure

- "You may need to try shipping 10 to 30 products for 1 to 3 years before you have anything that works."
- "If something doesn't seem to take off early on, it probably won't take off later, so make something new."
- He openly acknowledges survivorship bias: "the people who try and fail don't write books how they failed."

#### On Constraints as Advantages

- No money forces creativity; no team forces simplicity; no time forces focus on what truly matters.
- He credits not having a computer science degree for his pragmatic approach to technology choices.

## 4. Key Concepts Deep Dive

### 12 Startups in 12 Months Challenge

**Origin:** March 2014 blog post "I'm Launching 12 Startups in 12 Months"

**Method:** Every month, build and launch a project with Stripe payment integration.
Publish it as a blog post, put it on Hacker News, let the public hold you accountable.

**Results:** Most failed. Only 4 out of 70+ projects ever made money and grew.
The notable survivors: Nomad List, Remote OK.

**Philosophy:** Volume increases odds of success. Each project teaches something.
Public accountability creates motivation. Failure is the norm, not the exception.

### Building in Public

**Definition:** Sharing revenue numbers, tech decisions, failures, and wins publicly.

**Benefits:**
- Social accountability drives productivity
- Marketing through transparency (people share interesting numbers)
- Community building around shared values
- Attracts customers who appreciate honesty

**Method:** Post revenue screenshots, ship logs, decision rationale on Twitter/X.
Document building process in blog posts. Be honest about failures.

### Indie Hacking Philosophy

**Core beliefs:**
- Solo founders can build million-dollar businesses
- Bootstrapping beats VC for most founders
- Simple tech stacks ship faster
- Revenue validates ideas, not user counts
- Freedom and fun matter more than scale
- Constraints are advantages, not limitations
- Execution matters more than ideas
- Building is more valuable than talking about building

### Tech Stack Philosophy

**Stack:** PHP, jQuery, SQLite, single files, minimal infrastructure.

**Reasoning:**
- "PHP just stays the same and works"
- SQLite is "faster than the filesystem" for most use cases
- jQuery remains sufficient for most frontend needs
- Single PHP files can generate $100K+/month
- Over-engineering is the disease of modern developers
- The language doesn't matter; shipping matters
- "The people discussing what programming language is best are not shipping products"

**Anti-patterns:**
- Learning new frameworks before validating ideas
- Using complex stacks for simple products
- Hiring developers before having revenue
- Following trends in developer tooling

### Revenue Philosophy

**Math:** 1,000 customers x $83/month = $1M/year.

**Approach:**
- Charge from day one (free products don't validate demand)
- Be honest about needing money
- Don't sell user data or plaster ads
- Premium products for premium markets (Switzerland, UAE, US, Singapore)
- Niches are shallow enough to access with limited resources

### Solo Founder Model

**Advantages:**
- Ship faster without coordination overhead
- Keep 100% of profits
- Make decisions instantly
- Stay close to the product and users
- No politics, no meetings, no consensus-building

**Automation:**
- Automate repetitive tasks with code
- AI enables the first billion-dollar single-founder company
- Goal: 100% automation of routine operations
- Manual work first, automate what proves worth automating

### EU Criticism (Recurring Theme)

**Core argument:** EU overregulation stifles innovation and talent retention.

**Specific criticisms:**
- GDPR creates compliance burden without proportional benefit
- AI Act will strangle European AI development
- ChatControl threatens privacy
- Energy policy drives costs up unnecessarily
- AC bans and temperature regulations are paternalistic
- Tax systems discourage hard work
- Europe can't retain talent -- regulations and labor laws push founders to US, Dubai, Bali

**Reform vision:** EU/acc (European effective accelerationism) -- a counter-movement to EU degrowth policies.
