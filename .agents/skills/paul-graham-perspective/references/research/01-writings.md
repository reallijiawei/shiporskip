# Paul Graham Writings Research

## Overview

Paul Graham (b. 1964) is a programmer, essayist, investor, and co-founder of Y Combinator. His essays on paulgraham.com span 2001-2025 and cover startups, programming, thinking, wealth, design, and life philosophy. He has published 230+ essays, three books, and numerous tweets on X (@paulg).

- Website: https://paulgraham.com
- Essays index: https://paulgraham.com/articles.html
- X: https://x.com/paulg

## Books

### Hackers & Painters (2004)
A collection of essays exploring the connection between programming and other creative disciplines. Core argument: hackers are makers, not scientists, and programming is closer to painting than to mathematics. Topics include wealth creation, programming languages, the gap between designers and implementers, and why nerds are unpopular. [https://paulgraham.com/hp.html](https://paulgraham.com/hp.html)

### On Lisp (1993)
Advanced techniques for Common Lisp, focusing on macros and bottom-up programming. A technical reference for Lisp programmers. Available free at [https://paulgraham.com/onlisp.html](https://paulgraham.com/onlisp.html)

### ANSI Common Lisp (1995)
An introduction to Common Lisp for programmers. Two sample chapters available at paulgraham.com (acl1.txt, acl2.txt).

## Self-Coined Terms and Concepts

### 1. "Do Things That Don't Scale"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/ds.html](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html) (July 2013)

Startups don't take off on their own. Founders must actively make them succeed through laborious, unscalable efforts at the beginning. Graham uses the metaphor of a hand-crank engine: once running, momentum carries it forward, but someone has to crank it first.

**Key techniques:**
- **Recruit users manually.** Go find them, don't wait. "Collison installation" (Stripe brothers grabbing laptops and setting people up).
- **Delight your users.** Wufoo sent each new user a handwritten thank-you note. "I have never once seen a startup lured down a blind alley by trying too hard to make their initial users happy."
- **Be your software.** Do by hand what you plan to automate later. Stripe delivered "instant" merchant accounts by manually signing them up behind the scenes.
- **Big launches don't work.** "There may be an inverse correlation between launch magnitude and success." What matters is how happy you made early users.

**The vector framing:** Startup ideas are vectors (what you build + the unscalable actions to get started), not scalars.

**Core quote:** "You have to make an extraordinary effort initially. Any strategy that omits the effort... is ipso facto suspect."

### 2. "Default Alive or Default Dead"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/aord.html](https://paulgraham.com/aord.html) (October 2015)

Given your current trajectory (expenses, revenue, growth rate), would you reach profitability before running out of money, assuming no new funding?

- **Default Alive:** Will reach profitability before running out of money.
- **Default Dead:** Will run out of money before becoming profitable, even if things continue as they are.

**Core quote:** "Half the founders I talk to don't know whether they're default alive or default dead."

**The fatal pinch:** Default dead + slow growth + insufficient time to fix it. Founders end up there by not seeing it coming.

**The #1 killer:** "Don't hire too fast. Hiring too fast is by far the biggest killer of startups that raise money."

**The Airbnb example:** Airbnb waited four months after fundraising before hiring their first employee.

**Core quote:** "Instead of starting to ask too late whether you're default alive or default dead, start asking too early."

### 3. "Schlep Blindness"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/schlep.html](https://paulgraham.com/schlep.html) (January 2012)

The unconscious mind prevents founders from seeing startup ideas that involve tedious, unpleasant work ("schleps" from Yiddish). Founders don't reject hard ideas -- they simply never perceive them.

**Core quote:** "Your unconscious won't even let you see ideas that involve painful schleps."

**The Stripe example:** For over a decade, every developer knew online payments were painful. Thousands recognized the problem, yet "they decided to build recipe sites, or aggregators for local events." Fixing payments meant dealing with banks, fraud, regulations -- so the idea was invisible.

**The paradox of ignorance:** Young founders succeed because "they make two mistakes that cancel each other out" -- they underestimate both their own capacity and the difficulty ahead.

**Practical reframe:** Ask "what problem do I wish someone else would solve for me?" rather than "what problem should I solve?"

### 4. "The Other Half" (Distribution > Product)
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/artistsship.html](https://paulgraham.com/artistsship.html) (January 2009)

Organizations accumulate checks and approval processes over time, treating them as costless protections when they carry enormous hidden costs. The best people won't tolerate environments that prevent shipping.

**Core quote:** "People who propose new checks almost never consider that the check itself has a cost."

**Core quote:** "Artists aren't merely capable of shipping. They insist on it."

The "other half" of Jobs's maxim: the insight isn't just that capable people ship -- it's that people who insist on shipping won't tolerate environments that prevent it.

### 5. "Taste"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/taste.html](https://paulgraham.com/taste.html) (February 2002)

Taste is not subjective -- it's a real, cultivable skill essential for anyone who designs or creates things. Good design principles repeat across disciplines.

**The 15 principles of good design:**
1. Good design is simple
2. Good design is timeless
3. Good design solves the right problem
4. Good design is suggestive
5. Good design is often slightly funny
6. Good design is hard
7. Good design looks easy
8. Good design uses symmetry
9. Good design resembles nature
10. Good design is redesign
11. Good design can copy
12. Good design is often strange
13. Good design happens in chunks
14. Good design is often daring
15. (Implied) Good design is redesign

**Core quote:** "Very exacting taste, plus the ability to gratify it."

**Core quote:** "If taste can't improve, then there is no way to get better at your job."

### 6. "Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html](https://paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html) (July 2009)

Two fundamentally different ways to structure workdays:
- **Manager's Schedule:** Traditional appointment-book approach with one-hour intervals. Meetings are routine.
- **Maker's Schedule:** Programmers, writers, and creators prefer half-day units or longer. "Meetings are a disaster."

**Core quote:** "A meeting commonly blows at least half a day."

**Solution:** Office hours -- batch all meetings at the end of the working day so they never interrupt deep work.

### 7. "Mean People Fail"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/mean.html](https://paulgraham.com/mean.html) (November 2014)

Among highly successful people Graham knows -- particularly startup founders -- almost none are mean. Meanness and success are inversely correlated in innovation-driven fields.

**Core quote:** "Being mean makes you stupid."

**Core quote:** "Startups don't win by attacking. They win by transcending."

**Why:** Fighting requires narrow situational tricks rather than big, general ideas. Mean founders can't attract top talent. Benevolent motivation resists early acquisition offers.

### 8. "Frighteningly Ambitious Startup Ideas"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/ambitious.html](https://paulgraham.com/ambitious.html) (March 2012)

The most ambitious startup ideas repel potential founders rather than attract them. The best ideas feel intimidating because they threaten one's sense of identity and capability.

**Core quote:** "The biggest startup ideas are terrifying."

**The seven ideas:** New search engine, replace email, replace universities, internet drama, the next Steve Jobs, bring back Moore's Law, ongoing diagnosis.

**Tactical advice:** "The way to do really big things seems to be to start with deceptively small things." Gates started with a Basic interpreter; Zuckerberg started with Harvard undergrads.

### 9. "Relentlessly Resourceful"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/relres.html](https://paulgraham.com/relres.html) (July 2009)

The ideal startup founder quality distilled to two words. After years of trying to articulate what distinguishes good founders from bad ones.

**Core quote:** "A couple days ago I finally got being a good startup founder down to two words: relentlessly resourceful."

**Core quote:** "'Make something people want' is the destination, but 'Be relentlessly resourceful' is how you get there."

**Why both words:** Being merely relentless isn't enough -- startup obstacles are novel and unpredictable. Founders must adapt and try new approaches.

**The opposite:** "Hapless" -- a passive, reactive disposition where circumstances dictate outcomes.

### 10. "Life is Short"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/vb.html](https://paulgraham.com/vb.html) (January 2016)

Life is genuinely short, not just finite. The enemy is bullshit -- meetings, bureaucracy, pointless disputes, addictive pastimes. "The junk food of experience."

**Core quote:** "Relentlessly prune bullshit, don't wait to do things that matter, and savor the time you have."

**Core quote:** "Counterintuitive as it feels, it's better most of the time not to defend yourself."

### 11. "Startup = Growth"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/growth.html](https://paulgraham.com/growth.html) (September 2012)

The defining characteristic of a startup is rapid growth -- not technology, venture funding, or being newly founded.

**Core quote:** "A startup is a company designed to grow fast."

**Core quote:** "If there's one number every founder should always know, it's the company's growth rate."

**Growth rates:** 5-7% weekly is good, 10% is exceptional. 1% weekly yields 1.7x yearly; 5% weekly yields 12.6x yearly.

**Core quote:** "Small variations in growth rate produce qualitatively different outcomes."

### 12. "Founder Mode"
**Essay:** [https://paulgraham.com/foundermode.html](https://paulgraham.com/foundermode.html) (September 2024)

Two fundamentally different approaches to running a company: founder mode vs. manager mode. The conventional wisdom that scaling means switching to manager mode is damaging companies.

**Core quote:** "There are things founders can do that managers can't, and not doing them feels wrong to founders, because it is."

**Origin:** Brian Chesky spoke at a YC event about how the standard advice to "hire good people and give them room" led to disastrous results at Airbnb.

**Core quote:** "Founder mode will be more complicated than manager mode. But it will also work better."

## Key Essays: Detailed Summaries

### "How to Start a Startup" (2005)
[https://paulgraham.com/start.html](https://paulgraham.com/start.html)

Based on a YC lecture. Covers: launch fast, understand users, keep burn rate low, be ramen profitable, find a small market and dominate it, don't get distracted by deals and partnerships.

### "The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups" (2006)
[https://paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html](https://paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html)

Root cause: not making something users want. The 18 mistakes:
1. Single Founder
2. Bad Location
3. Marginal Niche
4. Derivative Idea
5. Obstinacy
6. Hiring Bad Programmers
7. Choosing the Wrong Platform
8. Slowness in Launching
9. Launching Too Early
10. Having No Specific User in Mind
11. Raising Too Little Money
12. Spending Too Much
13. Raising Too Much Money
14. Poor Investor Management
15. Sacrificing Users to (Supposed) Profit
16. Not Wanting to Get Your Hands Dirty
17. Fights Between Founders
18. A Half-Hearted Effort

**Core quote:** "It's easier to catch yourself doing something you shouldn't than always to remember to do something you should."

### "How to Get Startup Ideas" (2012)
[https://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html](https://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html)

**Core quote:** "Live in the future, then build what's missing."

Key principles:
- Solve real problems you have yourself
- Good ideas are "wells" (narrow but deep), not "craters" (broad but shallow)
- The verb is "notice," not "think up"
- Turn off filters: the "could this be a big company?" filter, the schlep filter, the unsexy filter
- The clash of domains is fertile ground
- Don't fear competition -- a crowded market signals demand

### "How to Do Great Work" (2023)
[https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html](https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html)

Four-step framework:
1. Choose a field you have aptitude for and deep interest in
2. Learn enough to reach the frontier of knowledge
3. Notice gaps -- things others overlook
4. Explore promising gaps

**Core quote:** "What are you excessively curious about -- curious to a degree that would bore most other people?"

**Core quote:** "Original ideas don't come from trying to have original ideas. They come from trying to build or understand something slightly too difficult."

### "Superlinear Returns" (2023)
[https://paulgraham.com/superlinear.html](https://paulgraham.com/superlinear.html)

Real-world returns are superlinear. Two root causes:
1. **Exponential growth:** Current performance compounds into future performance.
2. **Thresholds (winner-take-all):** Step functions where crossing a threshold yields outsized rewards.

**Core quote:** "Teachers and coaches implicitly told us the returns were linear."

**Core quote:** "If you're not learning, you're probably not on a path that leads to superlinear returns."

**Core quote:** "Ambition tends to make you climb existing peaks, but if you stick close enough to an interesting enough question, it may grow into a mountain beneath you."

### "How to Do What You Love" (2006)
[https://paulgraham.com/love.html](https://paulgraham.com/love.html)

Prestige and money derail people from finding work they love. "Prestige is just fossilized inspiration."

**The "Always Produce" heuristic:** "'Always produce' will discover your life's work the way water, with the aid of gravity, finds the hole in your roof."

**Core quote:** "Constraints give your life shape. Remove them and most people have no idea what to do."

### "Be Good" (2008)
[https://paulgraham.com/good.html](https://paulgraham.com/good.html)

Successful startups behave like nonprofits. Benevolence is a strategic advantage through three mechanisms:
1. **Morale:** Users are a "giant tamagotchi" -- once people depend on you, you're motivated.
2. **Help:** Good intentions attract supporters.
3. **Compass:** "Do whatever's best for your users. You can hold onto this like a rope in a hurricane."

**Core quote:** "Don't just not be evil. Be good."

### "How to Disagree" (2008)
[https://paulgraham.com/disagree.html](https://paulgraham.com/disagree.html)

The Disagreement Hierarchy (DH0-DH6):
- DH0: Name-Calling
- DH1: Ad Hominem
- DH2: Responding to Tone
- DH3: Contradiction
- DH4: Counterargument
- DH5: Refutation
- DH6: Refuting the Central Point

**Core quote:** "There is a lot more meanness down in DH1 than up in DH6."

### "Cities and Ambition" (2008)
[https://paulgraham.com/cities.html](https://paulgraham.com/cities.html)

Great cities send distinct messages:
- New York: "You should make more money."
- Cambridge/Boston: "You should be smarter."
- Silicon Valley: "You should be more powerful."
- Berkeley: "You should live better."
- Paris: "Do things with style."

**Core quote:** "A city speaks to you mostly by accident -- in things you see through windows, in conversations you overhear."

### "Keep Your Identity Small" (2009)
[https://paulgraham.com/identity.html](https://paulgraham.com/identity.html)

Religion and politics produce unproductive discussions because they become entangled with personal identity.

**Core quote:** "People can never have a fruitful argument about something that's part of their identity."

**Core quote:** "The more labels you have for yourself, the dumber they make you."

### "What We Look for in Founders" (2010)
[https://paulgraham.com/founders.html](https://paulgraham.com/founders.html)

Five essential qualities:
1. **Determination** -- the most critical trait, more important than intelligence
2. **Flexibility** -- determined to advance, but willing to go sideways
3. **Imagination** -- "In the startup world, most good ideas seem bad initially"
4. **Naughtiness** -- "a piratical gleam in their eye," rule-breaking spirit
5. **Friendship** -- co-founders must genuinely like each other

### "Founder Mode" (2024)
[https://paulgraham.com/foundermode.html](https://paulgraham.com/foundermode.html)

Two approaches to running a company: founder mode vs. manager mode. The standard advice to "hire good people and give them room" is often wrong for founders.

**Core quote:** "There are things founders can do that managers can't, and not doing them feels wrong to founders, because it is."

## Additional Essay Summaries with Direct Quotes

### "How to Make Wealth" (May 2004)
[https://paulgraham.com/wealth.html](https://paulgraham.com/wealth.html)

Originally published in Hackers & Painters. Wealth is not zero-sum; startups compress wealth creation through leverage and measurement.

**Key quotes:**
- "Wealth is stuff we want: food, clothes, houses, cars, gadgets, travel to interesting places, and so on."
- "You can make more wealth. Wealth has been getting created and destroyed for all of human history."
- "A programmer can sit down in front of a computer and *create wealth*."
- "To get rich you need to get yourself in a situation with two things, measurement and leverage."
- "If you want to make a million dollars, you have to endure a million dollars' worth of pain."
- "The same recipe that makes individuals rich makes countries powerful."

**Key concepts:** The Pie Fallacy (wealth is not fixed), measurement + leverage as the formula, technology as technique.

### "Do Things that Don't Scale" (July 2013)
[https://paulgraham.com/ds.html](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)

Early-stage founders must do labor-intensive manual work that wouldn't scale long-term but is essential for initial traction.

**Key quotes:**
- "Actually startups take off because the founders make them take off."
- "You can't wait for users to come to you. You have to go out and get them."
- "Your first users should feel that signing up with you was one of the best choices they ever made."
- "I have never once seen a startup lured down a blind alley by trying too hard to make their initial users happy."
- "Perfectionism is often an excuse for procrastination."
- "if you have to work hard to delight users when you only have a handful of them, you'll keep doing it when you have a lot."
- "you can provide a level of service no big company can."

**The vector framing:** Startup ideas should be thought of as "pairs of what you're going to build, plus the unscalable thing(s) you're going to do initially."

- "there is surprisingly little connection between how much a startup spends and how fast it grows."
- "the more you depend on it, the falser it becomes." (on assuming fundraising will be easy)
- "Investors are so fickle that you can never do more than start to count on them."

## Recurring Themes and Patterns

### On Starting
- Start with something you personally need
- Launch fast, iterate
- Do things that don't scale
- Be relentlessly resourceful
- Small teams win

### On Growth
- Growth rate is the defining metric
- 5-7% weekly is good, 10% is exceptional
- Small rate differences produce qualitatively different outcomes
- Superlinear returns reward exponential growth

### On People
- Determination > intelligence
- Benevolence is strategic
- Mean people fail
- Curiosity is the engine
- Environment shapes ambition

### On Work
- Maker's schedule vs. manager's schedule
- Do what you love (but be honest about what that is)
- Always produce
- Life is short -- prune bullshit

### On Thinking
- Keep your identity small
- Disagree at DH5 or DH6
- Taste is real and cultivable
- Notice, don't think up
- Live in the future, then build what's missing
