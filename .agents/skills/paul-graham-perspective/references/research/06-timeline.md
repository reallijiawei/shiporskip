# Paul Graham: Comprehensive Life & Career Timeline

## Timeline

| Year | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| **1964** | Born November 13 in Weymouth, Dorset, England | British-American; outsider perspective shapes his thinking on conformity and convention |
| **1968** | Family moved to Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania | Grew up in American environment; early exposure to computing culture |
| **1986** | BA in Philosophy from Cornell | Philosophy trained him to think from first principles rather than convention |
| **1986-1988** | Studied painting at RISD and Accademia di Belle Arti, Florence | Art education gave him the "maker's mindset" -- iterative, taste-driven work; basis of "Hackers & Painters" thesis |
| **1988** | MS in Computer Science from Harvard | Transition from humanities to technical field |
| **1990** | PhD in Computer Science from Harvard; thesis: "The State of a Program and Its Uses" | Focus on AI and Lisp; deep expertise in programming language design |
| **1993** | *On Lisp* published (Prentice Hall) | Advanced treatment of Common Lisp macros and bottom-up programming; crystallized belief that abstraction layers are the essence of good programming |
| **1995** | *ANSI Common Lisp* published (Prentice Hall) | Comprehensive reference; cemented his reputation in the Lisp community |
| **1995** | Co-founded **Viaweb** with Robert Morris; Trevor Blackwell joined shortly after | One of the first web-based applications; allowed users to build online stores via browser; written primarily in Common Lisp; often called the first SaaS company |
| **Summer 1998** | Viaweb sold to Yahoo for ~$49.6 million (455,000 shares); became **Yahoo! Store** | Financial independence; freed Graham to write and think without financial pressure |
| **1998-2005** | Post-Viaweb period: painting, writing, thinking | Used freedom to develop ideas about startups, technology, and culture that would become his essays |
| **Apr 2001** | Published **"Beating the Averages"** on paulgraham.com | First major essay; introduced the "Blub paradox" -- programmers using less powerful languages can't see what they're missing; argued Lisp gave Viaweb a competitive advantage |
| **2001** | Began publishing essays regularly on paulgraham.com | Started building the most influential essay collection in the startup world; eventually drew ~25 million page views per year |
| **2001** | Announced work on **Arc**, a new Lisp dialect | Began multi-year project to design his ideal programming language |
| **Feb 2002** | Published **"Taste for Makers"** | Extended his art/design thinking to engineering; argued good taste is learnable and essential for technical work |
| **Aug 2002** | Published **"A Plan for Spam"** | Proposed Bayesian filtering for spam; one of the most practically impactful essays; influenced the entire email industry |
| **Jan 2003** | Published **"Better Bayesian Filtering"** | Follow-up on spam essay; refined the approach |
| **May 2003** | Published **"Hackers and Painters"** (essay) | Core thesis: makers (hackers, painters, writers) share a mentality fundamentally different from managers and academics |
| **2004** | Published **"Hackers & Painters"** book (O'Reilly) | Collected essays into a coherent worldview; became required reading in Silicon Valley |
| **May 2004** | Published **"How to Make Wealth"** | Explained that startups are the best way to create wealth; reframed economics for a technical audience |
| **Mar 2005** | Published **"How to Start a Startup"** (essay/talk at Harvard Computer Society) | Practical startup advice distilled from Viaweb experience; became a foundational text |
| **Mar 2005** | Co-founded **Y Combinator** with Jessica Livingston, Robert Morris, and Trevor Blackwell | First of a new type of startup incubator; small investments ($15-20K) in batches of startups with intensive mentorship; fundamentally changed how early-stage startups are funded |
| **Summer 2005** | YC's first batch (S05): ~8 startups including **Reddit** and **Loopt** (Sam Altman's company) | Proved the batch model works; Reddit became one of the most popular sites on the internet |
| **2006** | Published **"How to Do What You Love"** | Essay on finding meaningful work; became one of his most widely shared pieces |
| **Winter 2007** | YC batch included **Dropbox** (W07) | Dropbox became a $10B+ company; validated YC's ability to identify exceptional founders |
| **Jan 2008** | Released **Arc** programming language | After years of development; a new Lisp dialect embodying his language design philosophy |
| **Mar 2008** | Published **"How to Disagree"** | Introduced the hierarchy of disagreement; one of the most widely cited essays on argumentation |
| **2008** | Married Jessica Livingston | Personal partnership that was also a professional one -- Livingston was YC co-founder and its operational backbone |
| **2008** | Named in *BusinessWeek*'s "25 Most Influential People on the Web" | Recognition of his outsized influence through essays and YC |
| **Winter 2009** | YC batch included **Airbnb** (W09) | Airbnb became a $80B+ company; the defining example of "do things that don't scale" |
| **Summer 2009** | YC batch included **Stripe** (S09) | Stripe became a $50B+ company; brothers Patrick and John Collison exemplified Graham's ideal of technical founders |
| **Jan 2009** | Published **"Keep Your Identity Small"** | Essay on identity and beliefs; prescient commentary on tribalism in tech and politics |
| **Jul 2009** | Published **"Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule"** | Explained why meetings destroy programmer productivity; became a touchstone for engineering culture |
| **2010** | Published **"What Happened to Yahoo"** | Post-mortem of Yahoo's decline; lessons for large companies; showed his ability to analyze organizational failure |
| **2010** | Published **"The Acceleration of Addictiveness"** | Early warning about technology's increasing addictiveness; prescient given later social media debates |
| **2011** | Published **"Do Things That Don't Scale"** | Perhaps his most influential startup essay; argued founders should do unscalable things to get initial traction |
| **Late 2011** | Banned SOPA-supporting companies from YC Demo Day | Political stance showing YC's willingness to use its influence; early example of tech industry political engagement |
| **2012** | Published **"Startup = Growth"** | Defined what a startup actually is: a company designed to grow fast; clarified the distinction between startups and small businesses |
| **2012** | Published **"Black Swan Farming"** | Honest essay about the extreme variance in startup returns; most YC companies fail, but the outliers make everything worthwhile |
| **Oct 2012** | Published **"How Y Combinator Started"** | Detailed history of YC's founding; revealed the accidental nature of many pivotal decisions |
| **2013** | YC batch included **DoorDash** (S13) | DoorDash became a $50B+ company; demonstrated YC's continued hit rate at scale |
| **Summer 2013** | Published **"How to Get Startup Ideas"** | Practical framework: look for problems you personally have; don't start with a company, start with a problem |
| **Oct 2013** | Published **"Startup Investing Trends"** | Analysis of how venture capital was changing; predicted the rise of smaller, faster funding rounds |
| **Jan 2014** | Published **"Mean People Fail"** | Controversial essay arguing that niceness correlates with startup success; challenged "tough guy" founder stereotypes |
| **Feb 2014** | Stepped back from day-to-day YC operations; **Sam Altman** became president | Handed operational control to Altman (a YC alum from W05); remained advisor; reflected belief that organizations need fresh leadership to scale |
| **Oct-Nov 2014** | Stanford CS183B: "How to Start a Startup" lecture series | Organized by Sam Altman; Graham gave "Before the Startup" lecture; other speakers included Peter Thiel, Marc Andreessen, Reid Hoffman; lectures widely shared on YouTube |
| **2015** | Published **"Default Alive or Default Dead?"** | Calculated whether startups would survive on current trajectory; forced founders to confront hard math about burn rate and growth |
| **2015** | Published **"Economic Inequality"** | Essay distinguishing between different types of inequality; nuanced take that challenged both left and right |
| **2015** | Published **"The Refragmentation"** | Analysis of how American society was fragmenting along many dimensions; sociological essay showing broader intellectual ambition |
| **2016** | Moved back to England with his family | Return to country of birth; continued writing from England |
| **2016** | Published **"Life is Short"** | Personal essay about the finitude of life; shifted toward more philosophical, introspective writing |
| **2017** | Published **"The Lesson to Unlearn"** | Essay about how school teaches you to hack tests rather than learn; anti-conventional-education stance |
| **2019** | Published specification for **Bel**, a new Lisp dialect written in itself | Culmination of decades of language design thinking; deeply personal project |
| **Nov 2019** | Published **"The Bus Ticket Theory of Genius"** | Essay on how obsessive interest in seemingly trivial things drives great work; connected to his views on curiosity vs. ambition |
| **2020** | Published **"Coronavirus and Credibility"** | Early pandemic essay about trust in institutions; showed willingness to comment on non-tech topics |
| **2020** | Published **"The Airbnbs"** | Essay about Airbnb's origin story; case study in persistence and unconventional thinking |
| **2021** | Published **"Is There Such a Thing as Good Taste?"** | Returned to aesthetics/philosophy; argued taste is real and learnable, not merely subjective |
| **2021** | Published **"How People Get Rich Now"** | Data-driven essay on modern wealth creation; showed shift from inherited wealth to startup wealth |
| **2022** | Published **"How to Work Hard"** | Essay on the nature of productive effort; distinguished between working hard and appearing to work hard |
| **2022** | Published **"A Project of One's Own"** | Essay on autonomy and ownership as drivers of great work; connected to his views on startups vs. corporate jobs |
| **Jul 2023** | Published **"How to Do Great Work"** | His longest essay (~8,000 words); fullest expression of his philosophy of work and achievement; synthesizes decades of observation about curiosity, taste, persistence, and the intersection of fields |
| **Oct 2023** | Published **"Superlinear Returns"** | Essay on how returns in wealth, reputation, and knowledge are superlinear; small differences in input produce disproportionately large differences in output |
| **Sep 2024** | Published **"Founder Mode"** | Went massively viral; distinguished "founder mode" (hands-on, cross-hierarchical) vs. "manager mode" (delegate to reports); challenged conventional advice to founders; sparked widespread debate in tech |
| **2024** | Published **"Writes and Write-Nots"** | Essay on AI making it obvious who can actually write vs. who relies on AI; commentary on AI's impact on writing |
| **2024** | Published **"The Origins of Wokeness"** | Controversial cultural essay; showed continued willingness to tackle non-tech topics; generated significant debate |
| **2024-2025** | Active on X (Twitter) commenting on AI, startups, and culture | Shifted some influence from essays to social media; frequent commentary on AI's implications for programming, writing, and startups |
| **2025** | Continues writing essays and advising founders | Focus on fundamental questions: what makes good work, how to think clearly, what matters; essays still draw millions of page views |

## The Arc of His Thinking

| Period | Focus | Key Insight |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| 1964-1990s | Education & Lisp | Languages matter; art + tech are the same discipline |
| 1995-1998 | Viaweb | Small teams + powerful tools beat big companies |
| 2001-2004 | Early Essays | Articulated principles: Blub paradox, hackers/painters, spam filtering |
| 2005-2014 | YC Growth | Fund people, not plans; do things that don't scale; startup = growth |
| 2014-2020 | Philosophical Writing | Life is short; economic inequality; how society fragments |
| 2020-present | Synthesis & AI Era | Great work, superlinear returns, founder mode; AI's impact on everything |
